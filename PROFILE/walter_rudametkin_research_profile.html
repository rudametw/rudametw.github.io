Research Landscape

Modern software systems are increasingly complex and diverse. While this diversity can improve resilience, it also introduces unique identifying characteristics. My work quantifies this "Software Diversity" to understand its implications for online privacy, specifically through techniques like browser fingerprinting.

Primary Research Vectors

A multi-disciplinary approach combining rigorous Software Engineering principles with Web Security and Privacy analysis.

Academic Impact Trend

Growing recognition of Software Diversity as a critical factor in privacy, evidenced by rising citations over the last decade.

The Reality of Browser Fingerprinting

Through the AmIUnique.org platform, we have collected over one million browser fingerprints. The data reveals a startling truth: the combination of your browser version, OS, fonts, and hardware creates a nearly unique identifier, allowing trackers to follow you without cookies.

How unique are users?

Percentage of visitors to AmIUnique who present a fingerprint that has never been seen before in our dataset.

90% of collected fingerprints are unique

Where does the diversity come from?

Uniqueness is not a single data point, but the accumulation of entropy across the entire software stack. Each layer adds information that distinguishes a user.

Layer 1
Hardware & Device
CPU Cores, GPU Renderer, Screen Resolution
Layer 2
Operating System
OS Platform, System Fonts, Default Languages
Layer 3
Browser Environment
User Agent, HTTP Headers, Plugins, Timezone
Result
Unique Fingerprint Identifier
